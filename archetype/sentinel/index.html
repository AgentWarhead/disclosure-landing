Designation 01 · Primary protector

The Sentinel holds the line so everyone else can think.

You put yourself between the unknown and everyone else, and you do it before anyone asks. This file covers what that instinct does in the first minute, where it goes wrong, and how to train it.

What a Sentinel is.

The Sentinel is defined by position, not aggression. When something unexplained happens, a Sentinel turns toward it and puts themselves between it and the people nearby. They rarely freeze, and they are usually the first to set a simple rule in a room that has none: stay behind me, stay together.

That makes the Sentinel the most physically present role in the first minute, and the one most likely to raise the temperature by accident. A Sentinel is measured by what they let happen safely behind them.

The first minute, as a Sentinel.

Five moves, in order. None of them is an approach.

  1. Count heads.

    Know who is here, who is missing, and who cannot move on their own.

  2. Find the gap.

    Pick the one spot between the light and your people, and stand in it.

  3. Give one order.

    “Stay behind me.” One short line, said once, at a normal volume.

  4. Keep your hands visible.

    Open palms, held low. Nothing sudden, no pointing, nothing in your hands that looks like a weapon.

  5. Hold. Do not advance.

    Your job is the line, not the approach. Let the Diplomat speak and the Survivor move people out.

What a Sentinel gets right.

  • Turns toward the problem while other people freeze.
  • Takes a position without waiting to be asked.
  • Brings order fast: one rule, one line, everyone knows where to stand.
  • Stays upright under pressure that sends other people to the floor.

The blind spot.

The Sentinel’s instinct fires before the facts arrive. Stillness looks like a threat, a sudden light looks like an attack, and a raised voice or one step forward can turn a strange minute into a dangerous one.

How to train it

Rehearse restraint until it is automatic: hands open and low, weight back, one step behind where your instinct wants to be. Practise holding a position for a full minute without moving toward anything. A line only works while it stays a line.

Working with the other three.

Somebody holds the space, somebody speaks, somebody records, somebody gets people out. No role outranks another.

Questions about the Sentinel.

What is the Sentinel role in DISCLOSURE?

The Sentinel is one of four civilian contact roles. It is the primary protector: the person who puts themselves between something unexplained and the people nearby, and holds that position calmly without escalating.

How do I know if I am a Sentinel?

Take the ten-question classification. Each answer leans toward one of the four roles, and the role you lean toward most is yours. Sentinels tend to choose the answers that stand in front: nobody goes alone, stay behind me.

Is the Sentinel the strongest role?

No role outranks another. A Sentinel without a Diplomat has a line and nothing to say through it. A Diplomat without a Sentinel is standing in the open. The four only work together.
