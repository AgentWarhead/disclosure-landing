Designation 04 · Self-preservation

The Survivor finds the way out and takes people through it.

You read the exit before you read the room, and your people are already moving. This file covers what that instinct does in the first minute, where it goes wrong, and how to train it.

File DSC-R04 / Survivor dossier / Released in partReleased in partCivilian copy

What a Survivor is.

The Survivor is not defined by fear. When something unexplained happens, a Survivor has already found the doors and decided who leaves first: the people they love, and anyone who cannot move on their own.

That is not cowardice. A room with fewer people in it is easier to keep calm, gives the Diplomat space, and gives the Sentinel less to cover. The risk is timing. Leave too early and you spread panic. Leave too late and your people are still in the room.

The first minute, as a Survivor.

Five moves, in order. Nobody runs unless there is a reason to.

  1. Find two ways out.

    The obvious door, and the one nobody else is looking at.

  2. Pick your people.

    Children, pets, older relatives, anyone frozen. Know exactly who you are taking.

  3. Move them quietly.

    Low voice, a hand on a shoulder, walking pace. Running starts a stampede.

  4. Get to cover.

    Not just away. A wall and a roof beat an open field.

  5. Count at the other end.

    Everyone you took and everyone who followed. Tell the Sentinel who is clear.

What a Survivor gets right.

  • Maps the exits in any room without trying.
  • Moves early and rarely freezes.
  • Puts the people who cannot help themselves first.
  • Keeps a household together under pressure.

The blind spot.

The Survivor’s exit instinct can fire before anyone knows what is happening. A sudden move toward the door can start the stampede it was trying to avoid, and leaving without a word leaves the rest of the room without a head count.

How to train it

Practise the calm exit. Walk your household’s route out in the dark, slowly, with the lights off, until it takes no thought. Rehearse telling one person where you are going before you go.

Working with the other three.

Somebody holds the space, somebody speaks, somebody records, somebody gets people out. No role outranks another.

Questions about the Survivor.

What is the Survivor role in DISCLOSURE?

The Survivor is one of four civilian contact roles. It is the self-preservation specialist: the person who finds the exits first and moves children, pets and anyone frozen to cover, calmly and early.

Is the Survivor role cowardly?

No. Getting vulnerable people out of the room is one of the four jobs of the first minute. It gives the Diplomat a quieter room and the Sentinel fewer people to cover. The training is about timing, so leaving stays calm.

How do I know if I am a Survivor?

Take the ten-question classification. Each answer leans toward one of the four roles, and the role you lean toward most is yours. Survivors tend to choose the answers that clear a path and protect their people.
