How the classification decides.

The classification is ten scenario questions. The power is out, a light is hanging over the hills, someone in the room starts to panic. You pick what you would actually do.

Each answer leans toward one of the four roles. At the end, the role you leaned toward most is yours. If two roles are tied, your later answers break the tie.

There are no right answers and no score. A role is not a grade. It is the job you are most likely to reach for when nothing makes sense, which makes it the job worth training.

Your result comes with a serial number and an iris print built from your exact answers, so nobody else finishes with the same one.

First Contact is not one of the four, and nothing on this site explains it.

The first minute, together.

In any strange minute, somebody holds the space, somebody speaks, somebody records, and somebody gets people out. No role outranks another. Each one covers what the others cannot.
