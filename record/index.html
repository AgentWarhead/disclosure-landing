The short answer

The US government has looked at UFO reports, on and off, since 1947. The record runs from the Air Force projects Sign, Grudge and Blue Book, through the 2017 reveal of a Pentagon program, to hearings under oath, a records law and, in 2026, the Department of War’s PURSUE file releases.

None of the official reviews listed here found evidence of extraterrestrial technology. What changed is the venue: the question moved from late-night radio into sworn testimony, federal law and government archives. Where a popular claim gets the record wrong, the ledger says so in claims that do not hold up.

Claims that do not hold up

These travel fast. Most start from something real and bend one detail: the agency, the kind of document, or what it actually says. One describes an event we could not find at all.

  1. FALSE AS WORDED

    “Trump signed a UFO executive order in February 2026.”

    What the record shows

    On February 19, 2026 the President said in a Truth Social post that he would direct agencies to identify and release UAP files. That was a public statement. No presidential document on UAP, UFOs or alien life appears in the Federal Register, and a search of whitehouse.gov found none. What followed was real: the Department of War began releasing files on May 8, 2026.

    nbcnews.com (source, opens in a new tab)federalregister.gov search (source, opens in a new tab)

  2. FALSE AS WORDED

    “The National Archives launched UFO bulk downloads on May 8, 2026.”

    What the record shows

    May 8, 2026 was the Department of War’s first PURSUE release at war.gov/UFO. The National Archives’ UAP bulk download page is older: it was last updated on April 24, 2025, and it covers records the Archives already held.

    archives.gov (source, opens in a new tab)war.gov (source, opens in a new tab)

  3. FALSE AS WORDED

    “The UAP Disclosure Act became law.”

    What the record shows

    Not in full. A reduced version became law in the FY2024 defense bill in December 2023: it created the UAP Records Collection at the National Archives, without the review board. A fuller UAP Disclosure Act passed the House inside its FY2027 defense bill on July 22, 2026. As of September 28, 2026 we found no Senate passage and no final law.

    govinfo.gov (source, opens in a new tab)burlison.house.gov (source, opens in a new tab)breakingdefense.com (source, opens in a new tab)

  4. NOT FOUND

    “Congress held a UFO hearing on July 17, 2026.”

    What the record shows

    We found no congressional UAP hearing on that date. The House UAP task force’s only 2026 UAP action we found is its April 1 request for video records. The most recent House UAP hearing we can confirm was on September 9, 2025.

    oversight.house.gov (source, opens in a new tab)

  5. FALSE AS WORDED

    “The released UFO files confirm alien contact.”

    What the record shows

    The files are real, but no official statement says they show non-human technology. The Department of War describes them as unresolved cases, where the government could not determine what was seen, and says many have not yet been analyzed. AARO’s own reports found no evidence of extraterrestrial technology.

    war.gov (source, opens in a new tab)cbsnews.com (source, opens in a new tab)media.defense.gov (source, opens in a new tab)

How the ledger is checked

Every row names the event in one plain sentence and links to the source it rests on. We read that source before the row goes in. When an official site blocked our checks, we read an archived copy of the same official page and link the original.

Verified
A primary source we read confirms the date and the fact: the agency, the law, the committee record or the report itself.
Partly
Confirmed only by a major news outlet or a secondary source, or one detail such as the exact day is unconfirmed. The row says which.
False as worded
Something real happened, but the claim as people repeat it gets the event, the agency or the outcome wrong.
Not found
We looked in the official record and could not find the event the claim describes.
  1. Primary sources first: the agency, the law, the committee record, the report.
  2. A major news outlet only when no primary source can be read, and the row is then marked Partly.
  3. Encyclopedias and tracker sites help us find sources. They are never the source.
  4. Claims by witnesses and officials are written as claims. Official findings are written as findings.
  5. No row is dated to the day unless a source gives the day.
