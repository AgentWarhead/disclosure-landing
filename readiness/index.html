    What first contact readiness means.

    Readiness is behavior, not belief. You do not have to think contact is coming to see the point: the first minute of any strange, frightening event goes to whoever is standing there, and nobody official will arrive in time to run it for them.

    Untrained people tend to fail in the same few ways. They freeze, rush toward it, film it badly, talk over each other, and invent details afterward. Readiness is the opposite: fewer moves, cleaner facts, and everyone safer at the end.

    The drill above puts a clock on that. Ten seconds is not long enough to reason your way to the right answer, which is the point. What you choose under a clock is what you have already practiced.

    The four axes.

    What readiness is made of. The drill measures three of them. The fourth is found by the ten questions.

    • Composure

      Can you slow your own panic before it spreads to the people around you?

      Drill meter: calm

    • Distance

      Can you keep people safe without chasing it, crowding it or provoking it?

      Drill meter: safety

    • Evidence

      Can you record what happened without writing your conclusions into the record?

      Drill meter: evidence

    • Role

      Can you do your one job instead of trying to do everyone else’s?

      Found by the quiz

    How the report is graded.

    Every run starts at calm 72, safety 68 and evidence 41. Each call moves the meters, and running out of time costs all three. The score is the average of the three meters after the sixth scene.

    ScoreGradeWhat it means
    82 to 100Field readySteady, out of reach, and the record intact.
    66 to 81Useful under strainA few risky calls, still functional.
    48 to 65A liability under pressureBrave in places, sloppy in others.
    0 to 47Contact compromisedToo much noise, too little distance.

    Questions about readiness.

    What is first contact readiness?

    The ability to stay calm, keep your distance, keep a clean record and do one job during a strange event, before anyone official arrives. It is a set of habits, not a belief.

    How do I improve my first contact readiness?

    Rehearse. Run drills like this one, learn the six steps in the First Contact Briefing, and find the role you default to so you know what to train. The habits you have practiced are the ones that show up under stress.

    What does the drill score mean?

    It is the average of your calm, safety and evidence meters after six scenes. It is a training score for this drill only, not a measurement of you, and it changes every time you run it.

    The drill found your habits. Now find your job.

    Ten questions name the role you reach for first, and you leave with a card that carries it. The drill trains the reflex; the field tools handle the night itself.
