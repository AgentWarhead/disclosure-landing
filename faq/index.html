If something is in the sky

What to do in the first minutes, and what the record actually says.

What should I do if I see a UFO, UAP or something I cannot explain?

Stop and stay still for a moment. Move children and anyone who depends on you back and away from it. Keep your distance, do not follow it, and keep lights and noise low.

Then record plain facts: the time, the direction you were facing, how it moved, how long it lasted and who else saw it. Film only once everyone is safe. Most sightings turn out to be satellites, planes, drones or balloons, and a clean record is what lets anyone tell the difference.

The full protocol is in What to do if you see a UFO.

What should I never do in a contact incident?

Do not rush toward it. Do not shine a strong light at it or try to provoke a reaction. Do not leave anyone who depends on you to get better footage.

Do not add details you did not see, even when you feel sure. A report with gaps is more useful than a report with guesses.

What is first contact?

The first confirmed interaction between people and a non-human intelligence, by signal, craft or presence. It has not happened on the public record.

Has the government confirmed alien contact?

No. Since 2023 there have been sworn House hearings, a law ordering a UAP records collection at the National Archives, and batches of declassified files from the Department of War. Every official report so far, including the Pentagon’s AARO review and NASA’s 2023 study, found no evidence of extraterrestrial technology.

None of these documents confirm contact. They confirm the question is now official. The dated record, with a source for every entry, is on the home page.

What is a first contact protocol?

A plan for the first minutes: keep people safe, keep your distance, stay calm, give everyone a job and record what happened cleanly. The civilian version is in the first contact briefing.

Why do you say UFO, UAP and NHI?

People use different words for the same thing. UFO is the older term. UAP, short for unidentified anomalous phenomena, is the term the government uses now. NHI means non-human intelligence. We use all three so you find the answer whichever word you typed.

The classification

Ten questions, four roles, and one designation that is not issued.

What is the alien encounter quiz?

Ten questions about what you would do in the first minutes of a contact event. Your answers point to one of four roles, each a job for the first minute.

As you answer, an iris forms beside the questions, grown from your exact answers. It runs on the home page.

What are the roles?

Four roles are issued. The Sentinel protects and holds the gap. The Diplomat lowers the temperature. The Scholar records what can be proven. The Survivor reads the exits and gets people moving.

A fifth designation, First Contact, is sealed. Each role has a dossier on the roles page.

How rare is the First Contact designation?

We do not publish a number, because nobody has measured one. First Contact is not issued like the other four. It appears. How it appears stays in the sealed file.

Can I choose my role?

No. The role comes from your answers. If you retake the classification and answer differently, you may get a different role and a different iris.

What is the First Contact Card?

Your role, serial number and iris print on one card. You can save it as an image when the classification ends, or have it sent to your email.

In the app, the card will also carry your readiness score. The one you get today is the first issue.

What is UAP readiness?

How well you would hold up in the first minutes of something strange, before anyone official arrives: whether you stay calm, know your job, keep people back and record what happened. The readiness drill is a quick way to find out.

How do I improve my readiness?

Practice the basics until they are automatic. Slow your breathing. Stay still for ten seconds before you act. Know your role. Record plain facts instead of guesses. Run through it once with the people you live with.

The app, still in development, turns these habits into drills you run on your phone.

The app and this site

What DISCLOSURE is, what it is not, and what happens to your answers.

What is Disclosure?

A phone app that trains ordinary people for the first minutes of contact, with this site as its public file. It starts by finding your role, then trains it: calm under pressure, signals that read as no threat, and a plan for the household. More on the about page.

Is the Disclosure app out yet?

Not yet. DISCLOSURE is in development for iOS and Android, and no release date is set. If you finish the classification and send your card to your email, you hear when it opens.

Is Disclosure a game or a real preparation tool?

It is preparation told as a leaked government file, so part of it is story. The habits it trains are real and useful on any strange night: staying still, keeping people back, and telling a satellite train from something you cannot explain.

Think of it as a fire drill for an event that may never happen.

Is Disclosure connected to the government?

No. DISCLOSURE is not affiliated with any government, military, intelligence agency or emergency service. It is preparation content and entertainment, not official emergency guidance. In a real emergency, follow your local authorities.

What are the intel files?

An archive of plain answers to the questions people search at 2 am: what they saw, what to do next, and what the government has actually said. Browse the intel archive.

What happens to my answers and my email?

Your answers stay in your own browser. If you send your card to your email, we keep your email, role and serial to send the card and, later, launch news. Nothing else. You can unsubscribe from any email.

The details are in the privacy policy.

Where do I start if I am new?

Take the classification first. Then read your role’s dossier, the first contact briefing, and the intel files that match what you saw or what worries you.
