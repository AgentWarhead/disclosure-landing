A preparedness file for something that may never happen.

DISCLOSURE is a phone app, still in development, that trains ordinary people for the first minutes of contact. This site is its public file. Here is what it is, who it is for, and the rules it keeps.

What DISCLOSURE is.

DISCLOSURE is a civilian first contact preparation app for iOS and Android. It is in development, and no release date is set.

It starts with a classification: ten questions that find your role for the first minute, one of four jobs that keep a room from panicking. Then it trains that role. The front camera coaches your hands into gestures that read as no threat. Tones and flashlight pulses give you a first hello. Drills with strange audio and sudden light teach you to stay still. Your role, serial and readiness score live on a First Contact Card on your phone.

Until the app opens, this site holds the parts you can use today: the classification and your iris print, a dossier for each role, the first contact briefing, the readiness drill, and an archive of intel files.

Who it is for.

People who watched the hearings and wondered what they would actually do. People who are not convinced by any of it and still find the question hard to put down. Parents who want a plan for any strange night, whether the cause turns out to be a satellite train, a blackout, or something nobody can explain.

And the person awake at 2 am, phone in hand, typing what they just saw into a search bar. Most of this site is written for them.

Why prepare for something that may never happen.

Schools run fire drills in buildings that never burn. Nobody thinks the drill predicts a fire. It exists because in the first minute of a real emergency, people fall back on whatever they have practiced, and people who have practiced nothing tend to freeze.

We do not know that contact will ever happen. Nobody does. Every official report so far, from the Pentagon’s UAP office to NASA’s study team, found no evidence of extraterrestrial technology. DISCLOSURE does not ask you to think otherwise.

What it asks is smaller. Know your job. Keep your breathing slow. Keep people back. Record what you saw without adding to it. Those habits hold up on any strange night, including the far more likely ones.

What it is not.

  • OfficialDISCLOSURE is not affiliated with any government, military, intelligence agency or emergency service.
  • Emergency guidanceIt is preparation content and entertainment, not official emergency guidance. In a real emergency, follow your local authorities.
  • A verdictIt does not claim contact has happened. Reports of beings, craft and abductions are treated as reports.
  • A labelEach role describes a job for the first minute. What you do with the rest of the week is your own business.

The rules this file keeps.

  1. Every public-record claim is sourced. Hearings, laws and releases are dated and linked to the office that published them.
  2. No invented numbers. We do not publish how many people hold each role, because nobody has measured it. Any number on this site that moves is a real one.
  3. Reports stay reports. Sightings, beings and abductions are described as what people say they saw. Official findings are described as what they found.
  4. The app is described as it is. It is in development. No store badges until there is a store to go to.
  5. The sealed file stays sealed. The fifth designation is not issued. How it appears is NOT FOR RELEASEredacted.
