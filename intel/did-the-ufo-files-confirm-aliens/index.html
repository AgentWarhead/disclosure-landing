Did the UFO files confirm aliens?

No. Six releases of government UFO files came out in 2026, and not one says anything was non-human. Here is what they do show.

File 068 · Public record · 7 min read · Filed 29 Sep 2026

File INT-068 / The UFO files against the claims / UnclassifiedReleased in partCivilian copy

No, the UFO files did not confirm aliens. Since May 8, 2026 the Department of War has published six batches of UAP files at war.gov/UFO, under a program called PURSUE. The department calls them unresolved cases: reports where the government could not decide what was seen. No release statement says any object was non-human or extraterrestrial.

That does not make the files empty. They hold military videos, pilot reports, FBI interviews, NASA audio and papers going back to the 1940s, and some describe things nobody has explained. Most claims about these files go wrong in one move: they read “unexplained” as “alien”. This file checks eight of them.

Quick answer

The UFO files are real government records. They show that the government holds many reports it cannot explain, and that many files have not been analyzed. They do not show an official finding of alien life, recovered craft or contact. AARO, the Pentagon’s UAP office, says in its latest annual report that no evidence suggests anyone has ever captured or exploited UAP material.

What are the UFO files?

“The UFO files” almost always means the PURSUE releases. PURSUE stands for the Presidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP Encounters. The first release named the White House, ODNI, the Department of Energy, AARO, NASA and the FBI as partners (Department of War, May 8, 2026). It followed a February 19, 2026 Truth Social post in which the President said he would direct agencies to identify and release files on UAP, UFOs and alien life (NBC News).

The department’s own wording is the key to everything below. On war.gov/UFO it says the materials are unresolved cases, meaning the government is unable to make a definitive determination about what was observed, often for lack of data. It adds that resolved cases are reported separately, as the law requires (war.gov/UFO). The first release also said many of the materials “have not yet been analyzed for resolution of any anomalies.” How the program works is set out on the PURSUE files page.

What did the UFO files reveal?

Each release has its own case file. The counts are newsroom tallies, credited to the outlet that made them, because the department’s release pages list download sizes rather than file counts.

Eight claims about the UFO files, checked

Each verdict uses the same scale as the Disclosure Ledger. Unresolved means the record cannot settle the question either way.

FALSE AS WORDED

“The UFO files confirmed that aliens are real.”

No release statement says so. None of the six statements from the department makes any finding about where an object came from, and the department describes the whole archive as unresolved. An unresolved case is a case with no answer. That is different from a case with a non-human answer.

VERIFIED

“The government admits it cannot explain what is in the files.”

That is how the department describes the archive: cases where it cannot make a definitive determination, often because the data is thin (war.gov/UFO). CBS News also reported that several fifth-release incidents could not be resolved by AARO.

FALSE AS WORDED

“Everything in the files has been explained away.”

The opposite. The first release said many materials had not been analyzed for resolution at all, and the files were published because they are unresolved. For the ordinary causes that usually explain a sighting once there is enough data, see airplane, satellite, balloon or UFO?

FALSE AS WORDED

“The files prove the government covered up alien contact.”

NBC News reviewed the first release and reported that the documents do not suggest a wide-ranging cover-up and show “no indication” of any government interaction with beings from other planets (NBC News). That is a newsroom’s reading, not a government finding. No later release statement claims contact either.

FALSE AS WORDED

“The files show crashed or recovered alien craft.”

No release statement describes recovered craft or material. AARO’s fiscal 2025 report to Congress says no evidence suggests that a government or private entity has ever captured or exploited UAP-derived materials (AARO FY2025 report). Claims to the contrary come from witnesses and former officials, and they remain claims. The report itself is broken down in the AARO 2025 report explained.

FALSE AS WORDED

“The files were released because Trump signed an order.”

No executive order on UFOs exists. On February 19, 2026 the President said in a Truth Social post that he would direct agencies to identify and release the files, and the department cites that direction (NBC News). The full check is in the UFO executive order that was never signed.

FALSE AS WORDED

“The UFO files came from the National Archives.”

Different agency. PURSUE is run by the Department of War with support from ODNI. The National Archives runs a separate UAP Records Collection, Record Group 615, created under the 2024 defense law, with series from agencies such as the FAA, the FBI, the NSA and the Air Force (National Archives). The two are compared in the UAP Records Collection explained.

UNRESOLVED

“Something in the files could still turn out to be non-human.”

The record cannot settle this one. The department says it cannot determine what many of these things were. That leaves every explanation open, from balloons and satellites to sensor faults to something nobody has named, and it favors none of them. What would move a case is better data, which is exactly what most of these files lack.

Why unresolved does not mean alien

AARO’s fiscal 2025 report shows what happens to reports when there is enough to work with. Of 319 new reports, AARO resolved 114, every one as an ordinary object: balloons, satellites, birds, aircraft and drones, plus one commercial rocket launch and one person on a jet pack. It moved 191 to an “active archive” because the data was too thin to judge (AARO FY2025 report).

By the department’s own description, the PURSUE archive is made of the second kind: cases the government could not close. Publishing them is new. What they contain is the familiar problem of a sighting that lacks a time stamp, a second sensor and any way to measure distance. That is why a good report records the time, the direction and the sky, as the UFO evidence checklist sets out.

What would confirmation look like?

For the files to confirm aliens, an agency would have to say so in writing, with the data behind it. Every official document we read for this file points the other way. The department calls its archive unresolved. AARO’s latest report says none of the cases it resolved indicated breakthrough technologies in any domain. Until an agency says something different, a released file is a record of what someone saw, not a finding about what it was.

Common questions

Did the UFO files confirm aliens?

No. The Department of War has published six batches of UFO files since May 8, 2026, and describes them as unresolved cases. No release statement says any object was non-human or extraterrestrial.

What did the UFO files reveal?

Real government records: military videos, pilot and police reports, FBI interviews, NASA audio, and in the sixth release a set of DIA reference papers. Most describe sightings the government could not explain, often for lack of data.

How many UFO files have been released?

Six releases as of September 29, 2026. News counts per release range from 40 files to more than 160. The department’s release pages list download sizes rather than a file count.

Are the UFO files real?

Yes. They are published by the Department of War at war.gov/UFO. The Pentagon has warned that some materials lack a documented chain of custody, and that the sixth-release study papers do not validate the ideas they discuss.
